## Reading guide

* **Cache bandwidth** only together with the working-set size, and as a range: campaigns on the same Cache node differ by up to 15% for the same configuration (claim `campaign_spread`). It declines progressively from 8 GiB onward, well before the 64 GiB cache capacity; read-only and non-temporal Triad fall below Flat DDR at 56 GiB, Triad at 96 GiB. A conflict-miss cause is inferred, not measured. Past the capacity a decode GEMM streams weights faster than the read-only kernel, so large-model statements use the GEMM rate (claim `cache_spilled_gemm`).
* **NUMA bandwidth** with the CPU socket and memory locality: remote HBM is slower than remote DDR, confirmed by a repeat with 5 s windows (46.5 GB/s at 56 threads, about 9% of local); it is fastest with 14–28 threads. At 56 threads every remote HBM line is read about twice from memory and crosses UPI about twice; directory traffic and link saturation are ruled out (claim `remote_duplicate_reads`).
* **GEMM at small M** only from a converged weight pool: the first sweep's pool overstates the weight traffic at M ≤ 32 by 8–25% (claim `first_sweep_small_m_bias`); those points are drawn hollow.
* **Decode.** The read ceiling bounds decode; what the library achieves is lower on HBM (60–64% of the read ceiling) than on DDR (86%), so HBM beats DDR by 1.4–1.55× for a decode GEMM, not the 2.07× of the read kernel (Table 5, claim `decode_achieved`).
* **Latency** is a one-core dependent chase. Idle, it does not predict saturated bandwidth. Under load (one chasing core beside N streaming cores, claim `loaded_latency`) it rises from 139 to 209 ns on HBM and from 116 to 373 ns on DDR when 55 cores stream, and agrees with McCalpin's counter-measured queue occupancy (claim `literature_loaded`). The Flat DDR chase to 128 GiB rises by only 4 ns from 16 GiB, so the far latency of Cache mode (161 to 227 ns) is the memory-side cache, not translation (claim `latency_translation`).
* **Peaks versus GEMM.** A tile or vector kernel shows what the instruction can do; the library GEMM shows what representative matrix shapes reach.
* **Efficiency** is judged at the effective clock. Above 100% against base clock is a clock effect.
* **Clocks and power.** From about 28 cores the streams hold the package at its 350 W limit (PL1) and the clock is cut for power, more on HBM (1.7 GHz cores, 1.5 GHz uncore at 56 cores) than on DDR; HBM bandwidth follows the uncore clock, which is why the first one-second HBM window, run above PL1 after the idle setup, is faster (claims `clock_drop_power`, `first_window_uncore`). Compute peaks stay below the limit.
* **Measured intensity.** The roofline points of notebooks 00 and 05 come from counted re-runs: filled at the intensity the memory controllers measured (Flat) or the cores' DRAM line counts (Cache, until its node has perf), hollow at the modeled intensity. Streams agree with the model to 2% (claim `controller_streams`); GEMMs agree for decode-sized batches and not for prefill, where the library re-reads weights (claim `measured_intensity_gemm`). Rates elsewhere (Tables 1 and 3, the scaling figures) are in modeled bytes, with the measured value beside them in Table 1.
* **The memory-side cache is counted.** The hit rate of a 56-core stream falls from 92% at 8 GiB to 45% at the 64 GiB capacity and 12% at 128 GiB, the same for read, Triad and non-temporal Triad, and the rate follows it (claim `cache_hit_rate_curve`); DDR traffic starts at the smallest working set and the DRAM power follows it (`cache_dram_power_onset`); the spilled decode GEMM hits the same 25% as the streams and out-streams the read kernel because it keeps DDR busier with fills (`cache_spilled_gemm_counted`). The hit rate repeats to 2 points within a day and differs between days: the 3.5× difference near the capacity between the launches of 2026-10-20 and 2026-10-31 is a page-placement difference (`cache_launch_spread`), so a Cache capacity curve belongs to a node state. Counting the cache through perf's tag lookups slows the benchmark (`cache_tag_session_cost`); the base counters give the same hit rate (`cache_hit_rate_from_cas`).
* **The floor and the wall.** At rest both nodes draw 310–315 W of RAPL power and about 400 W at the wall; the BMC's CPU and memory rails equal RAPL's package and DRAM (claims `idle_floor`, `rapl_dram_is_ddr`); energy per byte, per operation and per token are also given dynamic, with the floor subtracted.
* **Two sockets at once** cost 1.2–1.4× the node power of one socket for twice the throughput; a decode token is 18.5 J at the node on Flat HBM when both sockets work, 28.0 J when one does (claim `dual_energy`).
* **Energy scopes.** Energy figures are at the computing socket or at the node (both sockets), package plus DRAM, idle floor included unless marked dynamic (docs/06_COUNTERS.md). The socket that runs nothing draws 131–235 W because its uncore clock follows the busy socket's, so node energy per byte is about 1.5× the computing-socket figure and the latter must not be doubled for two sockets (claim `idle_socket_uncore_coupling`). Flat HBM costs 18.2 J per decode token at the computing socket and 27.2 J at the node; Cache resident 1.21×, Flat DDR 1.61× and Cache spilled 2.97× that at the computing socket (1.19×, 1.57× and 3.01× at the node); work that touches no memory costs the same in both modes (claim `energy_flat_vs_cache`).
* **Remote HBM and power.** At 56 threads the serving socket of a remote HBM read draws about 345 W at its maximum uncore clock and is not clipped for power (0% of PCU cycles) while the computing socket is clipped in all of them; the counters show no power limit on the serving socket and say nothing about causation (claim `remote_duplicate_reads`).
* **Two sockets** are two independent processes (or one spanning process, reported separately); this is not tensor-parallel scaling.

Not inferred, not measured: the Cache-mode bytes of the parts not re-run with the Cache counters (`ustreams`, `ugemmconv`, `usweep`: the cores' DRAM line counts, 1% from the cache's served bytes for streams and decode GEMMs), SMT effects (SMT is off), the HBM-cache hit rate behind the far latency, causes of the variation between Cache-mode campaigns, and which agent issues the duplicated remote-HBM reads.
