# Scientific review — 2026-10-12

Independent review of the hardware characterization and roofline analysis, kept as a
reference for the follow-up work. It describes the repository at commit `99002b1`
(`develop`); findings may be resolved by later commits.

**Scope and method.** Review only: no files were changed and no cluster jobs were run.
Locally, `pytest` passed (48 tests); every headline number was re-derived from
`data/processed/measurements.csv` and the raw records; the compiled disassembly
(`system/hardware.asm`) and the oneDNN verbose logs in the GEMM records were inspected;
all 21 notebook figures were rendered to PNG outside the repository for visual review.
`scripts/check_artifacts.py` failed in that checkout because `figures/` was empty.

## Verdict

The compute characterization is thesis-grade now. Every ISA path except AVX-512 BF16
reaches its vendor or derived operations per cycle within 1–3.5% at the measured clock.
Run-to-run spread is under 2%, and Flat vs Cache and node vs node agree within 0.6%.

The Flat DDR and Flat HBM memory characterization is solid. The roofline as presented
is not yet defensible, for four reasons:

- Several measured points lie above "ceilings" that are described as upper bounds.
- The Cache-mode conclusion contradicts the repository's own data.
- The GEMM points that link the hardware to LLM decode and prefill have unresolved
  traffic and overhead confounds.
- The master notebook contains no roofline at all.

All of this can be fixed with mostly local analysis work plus about three short
cluster runs.

## 1. Scientific audit

### Strengths (keep these)

- **Execution guards.** Flat runs are pinned to `cresco8-hbm14` by construction. The
  Cache template excludes that node, and a test enforces the exclusion.
- **Synchronized dual windows.** Rates are summed work over the shared window, with an
  overlap rule.
- **Per-thread self-monitoring.** `cycles` and `EXE.AMX_BUSY` are read per thread via
  `perf_event_open`. Efficiency at the effective clock is the correct comparison
  against vendor figures.
- **Provenance.** Selection is explicit (`supersede`), failed runs are preserved, there
  is a cross-check campaign, and source hashes are recorded.
- **Correctness checks.** Every compute kernel checks exact integer or FP results.
- **Kernels are sound.** In the disassembly, the `vdpbf16ps` loop is 12 independent zmm
  chains, and `vpdpbusd` and the FMA loops are zmm.

## 3. Roofline assessment

**Kind of each quantity** (this split should appear explicitly in the thesis):

| Kind | Quantities |
|---|---|
| Theoretical | ISA ops/cycle (`theory.toml`); DDR 307.2 GB/s; HBM "up to 1 TB/s" (vendor, rounded); HMAT 262 / 869 GB/s (firmware-advertised, not measured) |
| Directly measured | Time, operation counts, user-mode cycles, `AMX_BUSY`, latency per load, checksums |
| Modeled | All bytes and GB/s, arithmetic intensity, GEMM traffic, ridge points, "decode tokens/s" bounds if added |
| Inferred (label as such) | No write-allocate (RFO) traffic on Triad; "resident in the HBM cache"; AMX clock limited by power; cause of the remote-HBM collapse; cause of the GEMM gap |

**Defensible now:**

- Compute peaks for 5 of 6 paths, including AMX at 98.8% of the peak at the measured clock.
- Flat DDR and HBM sustained bandwidth with 56 cores, 1S and 2S.
- Dual-socket independence: efficiency 0.98–1.02.
- DDR+HBM bandwidth is not additive: 483 GB/s combined vs 554 for HBM alone.
- Ridge points as order-of-magnitude statements: AMX BF16 ≈200 op/B on HBM, ≈430 on DDR; FP64 ≈8 op/B.
- The "no write-allocate" inference can be strengthened: if Triad paid read-for-ownership,
  true DDR traffic would be 32/24 × 235 = 313 GB/s, above the 307.2 GB/s theoretical peak.

**Needs correction:** Cache-mode statements (A1–A3); ceiling definition so measured points
are below their ceiling or the excess is explained (A4); meaning of the HBM ceiling (A8);
GEMM placement on the roofline (A5–A7); the FP64 sweep claim (A10).

**Cannot be established without counters:** real DRAM/HBM traffic of the GEMM, HBM-cache
hit rate, the remote-HBM mechanism (directory updates vs UPI), why the clock drops under
HBM streaming (power limit vs Energy Efficient Turbo), and energy.

## 4. Visualization audit

- **Master `compute_peaks`.** Bars on a log axis distort ratios. "Theoretical at 1.9 GHz"
  markers sit *below* the measured bars, inviting the wrong reading. Raw keys (`amx_bf16`)
  used as labels.
- **Palette collision.** `PATH_COLORS` reuses the DDR blue for FP64 and the HBM red for
  AMX BF16 ([plotting.py:39](../src/hwbench/plotting.py#L39)); in the atlases the AMX BF16
  ceiling reads as a continuation of the HBM slope.
- **Atlas figures.**
  - 12–23 traces with 3-row legends; theoretical lines nearly overlap measured ones.
  - Compute plateaus start at the HBM ridge ([plotting.py:104](../src/hwbench/plotting.py#L104)),
    so the DDR slope runs underneath every plateau up to 226 TOP/s.
  - Point symbols are assigned by group index ([plotting.py:148](../src/hwbench/plotting.py#L148)),
    so the same shape gets different symbols on DDR and HBM.
  - The x-axis spans 0.05–4096 with nothing plotted at either end.
- **`cache_capacity`.**
  - Read and NT Triad have only 8/16/64/96/128 GiB points; linear interpolation fakes a
    smooth curve between 16 and 64.
  - Default Plotly colors.
  - No Flat HBM or Flat DDR reference line, which is the comparison the figure should show.
- **`dual_socket_efficiency`.** Bars on a truncated y-axis (0.95–1.03) make 1% differences
  look large; no error bars although isolated runs have CV ≈3%.
- **`span_bandwidth`.** Blue/red reused for interleave/first-touch, which already mean
  DDR/HBM. The two-process reference is computed (`dual_ref`,
  [build_notebooks.py:530](../scripts/build_notebooks.py#L530)) but never plotted, yet the
  text claims that comparison.
- **`hierarchy_read_56cores`.** "L2 2 MiB" and "L3 share 2 MiB" labels overprint each
  other. The L3 share equals the L2 size, so this sweep cannot resolve L3 at all; say so.
- **Notebook 04 GEMM sweeps.** Default colors; log-log without error bars hides the
  bimodal M=64 points.
- **Tables.** Six decimals everywhere and no uncertainty columns.

**Recommended master narrative** (research question → evidence → observation → figure → conclusion):

1. Compute ceilings: operations per cycle per core vs vendor, as a linear dot plot
   annotated with the measured GHz.
2. Memory ceilings: bandwidth vs cores (DDR saturates at 28, HBM still rising at 56),
   effective clock on a secondary marker.
3. Cache mode vs working set: all kernels, Flat HBM and Flat DDR as reference bands,
   markers at 16 GiB (Llama-3.1-8B BF16 weights) and 64 GiB.
4. One headline roofline: Flat, one socket. Read-only HBM and DDR slopes; AMX BF16, AMX
   INT8 and FP32 ceilings; a dotted library-achievable ceiling; the 14336×4096 BF16
   M-sweep labelled by M, with decode (M≤16) and prefill regions shaded.
5. GEMM efficiency vs M on twin axes: fraction of tile peak and fraction of read bandwidth.
6. Placement: a compact local/remote plus span panel.

Everything else stays in notebooks 02–05.

## 5. Experimental gaps, ranked by scientific value

1. **GEMM validity runs** (Flat, ≈1 h):
   - Pool sweeps at 4×, 16×, 64× of L2+L3 until the M=1 bandwidth converges.
   - A tiny-shape baseline (e.g. 256²) to measure the fixed per-call cost.
   - BF16 with prepacked weights, or `benchdnn`, to remove Python and packing overhead.
   - More warm-ups.

   Settles H1 and H2, which carry the LLM link.
2. **Cache-mode working-set fill-in** (one Slurm hour): read and NT Triad at
   24/32/40/48/56 GiB, plus pointer chase at 32, 96 and 128 GiB. Fixes C2 and supplies the
   missing miss latency.
3. **Remote-HBM repeat:** separate campaign, 5 s windows, 1–56 thread sweep. Confirms or
   retracts the 10× claim.
4. **AVX-512 BF16 chain sweep** (6, 12, 24 chains; minutes) plus a citation from uops.info
   or Intel's instruction tables. Settles A13.
5. **FP64 sweep** with 12 chains up to K=1024, to show the plateau and the ridge region.
6. **Independent repeats** (three launches) of the six headline numbers for between-run
   uncertainty; `xcheck` already partly covers this.

Not recommended now: more shapes, more thread counts, SNC4 work. SNC4 or HBM-only belongs
in future work: quadrant mode plausibly explains the gap to the 869 GB/s HMAT figure, but
nothing here measures that.

## 6. Counter-access roadmap

**Available now** (`perf_event_paranoid=2`, per thread, user mode):

- Extend `Counters` with `instructions` and `CPU_CLK_UNHALTED.REF_TSC` (frequency ratio).
- Add L3-miss and off-core-response (OCR) events with supplier bits as a per-core proxy for
  DRAM traffic. Verify first that `perf list` on hbm14 exposes them and that they open
  unprivileged.

**Indirect proxies, defensible now:** pool-size and working-set convergence sweeps;
like-for-like kernel comparisons; the theoretical-bound argument for write-allocate
(Section 3); bandwidth vs threads together with `ghz_effective`.

**Future, requires an admin** (CAP_PERFMON or paranoid ≤ 0, or likwid's access daemon):

- `uncore_imc` CAS counts for real DDR/HBM bytes and actual intensity.
- Mesh-to-memory (M2M) near-memory tag hit/miss events for the Cache-mode hit rate (check
  event names in Intel's perfmon tables).
- `uncore_cha` and `uncore_upi` for the remote-HBM mechanism.
- `power/energy-pkg`, energy-ram and `turbostat` for energy and the cause of the clock drop.

The PMU types `uncore_imc`, `uncore_cha` and `power` are already listed in
`platform.json`; only permissions block them.

**Integration needs no redesign.** `WorkloadPoint.bytes_source` already allows
`"pmu_l3miss"`. Add `"imc_cas"`, store counted bytes per sample next to `modeled_bytes`,
add a column in `process.py`, and draw counted vs modeled intensity as paired markers. A
single privileged `perf stat -a` session over the GEMM and sweep points would validate the
traffic model.

## 7. Thesis extraction proposal

**Figures:** the six master figures above, plus one methods figure (dual/repeat validation
as a dot plot with ranges).

**Tables:**

- **T1:** headline ceilings as median [min–max] plus cross-run Δ, with the quantity kind.
- **T2:** ridge points per path × tier.
- **T3:** measured vs theoretical vs firmware-advertised (HMAT) values.
- **T4:** what is not measured, and why.

**Conclusions** in their defensible form:

- AMX delivers 99% of its per-cycle peak, but the AMX clock is ≈2.0 GHz against ≈2.5 GHz
  for AVX-512.
- 56 cores draw ≈550 GB/s from HBM per socket (2.1–2.4× DDR, depending on kernel); still
  concurrency-limited.
- Cache mode loses bandwidth progressively with working-set size: an 8B BF16 model
  (≈16 GiB) keeps ≈86–95% of Flat HBM bandwidth, larger models do not.
- Decode is ≈200× below the AMX ridge.
- Worth adding a modeled decode bound: 543 GB/s ÷ 16 GB ≈ 34 tokens/s per socket on HBM
  vs 16 on DDR.
